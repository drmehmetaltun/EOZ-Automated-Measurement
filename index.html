Centration & optical zone

About Px/Py and thresholds
Px/Py is the applied centration offset entered by the surgeon, with your marked pupil center as origin: TC = pupil + (Px, Py). If Px=Py=0, the target is the pupil center (TC = pupil). When the offset is fully applied, TC approaches the corneal vertex (map origin); the residual is reported as residual centration gap. TC is the intended point; the point where the laser actually landed is the measured ablation center.
Default: +Px rightward, +Py UPWARD (above vertex is positive) — matches the internal mm coordinate frame. If your device uses a different sign convention (e.g. +Py inferior or +Px temporal), toggle the corresponding button. Verify on the first real case that the TC marker moves in the expected direction.
Ablation center = EOZ center = achieved treatment center. Ellipse fit is the common definition in the literature (Meng 2025 fitting ellipse; some studies use best-fit circle). Area centroid makes no shape assumption. If they diverge, the zone is irregular and the ellipse assumption weakens — the difference is reported below.
Boundary definition
The boundary is always the MID-LINE of the 0 D bin. The default method is radial interpolation: on each semi-meridian, the last sample above and the first sample below the target bin are found, and the boundary is placed at their midpoint. This does not require the bin to be VISIBLE in the image — it works even when bin width is zero pixels. Bin width is also computed analytically from the local gradient (width = step / |dD/dr|). If this path fails, a two-mask bin-midline approach is used as fallback.
The «inner/outer edge» option from earlier versions has been removed. Measurement: inner and outer contours on a real map sit at +0.132 D and −0.132 D (expected ±step/2 = ±0.125), while the midline yields −0.001 D with 84% of points inside the 0.00 D bin. Edge mode introduces a systematic bias of roughly band/2 in radius — measuring an adjacent bin instead of 0 D.
When the band cannot be resolved, the program falls back to edge mode by necessity; this is a degraded measurement, flagged in the result panel and marked as boundary_mode = edge in CSV — these eyes should be excluded from analysis.

Batch analysis

—
Up to 500 images — covers 250 patients with both eyes. Filename is used as patient/eye ID (extension stripped). Each image is independently calibrated, scale is read, and the 0.00 D iso-contour is extracted.
Time: approximately 0.5–2 seconds per image; a 500-image batch takes a few minutes. Estimated time remaining is shown next to the progress bar. Stop preserves rows completed so far. Keep the tab in the foreground; background tabs throttle the timer.
Produces the same result as individual analysis — same origin rules and same preprocessing. Only manual interventions are not carried to batch mode: manually marked vertex, manually entered px/mm scale, and pupil marking. If you corrected these for an eye, its batch result will differ; analyze that eye individually and add it to the series.
Important limitation: pupil center is marked manually and cannot be marked in batch mode. Therefore the batch report contains only vertex-referenced decentration (decCV). Pupil/target center referenced decTC and Px/Py offset are not computed — open the eye individually and mark the pupil for those.
Failed images are not skipped but listed with the realast in the report.
Privacy: filenames appear in the report as-is; rename files with anonymous IDs before sharing.

How values are computed

Calibration and coordinate frame

Scale (px/mm)
Derived from the vertical ruler bar next to the map. Consecutive tick marks are 1 mm apart; the median spacing is used to determine the pixel/mm ratio.
Corneal vertex (map origin)
The black dot inside the white ring; detected as the center of mass of the dark component closest to the ring center. All mm coordinates are relative to this point.
Terminology. Pentacam labels this point apex in its interface. However, since the device does not contain a Placido disc, this point is coaxial with the fixation target — i.e. the corneal vertex (vertex normal) corresponding to the coaxially sighted corneal light reflex (CSCLR), not the geometric curvature apex (Chang & Waring, Am J Ophthalmol 2014;158:863–874). The program therefore uses corneal vertex from v6 onward.
Dual decentration. The vertex is not only a common reference frame but also an anatomical landmark. Therefore two values are reported: decTC = |ablation center − TC|  ·  decCV = |ablation center − vertex| decTC measures how accurately the treatment center landed on the intended point (technical fidelity: eye tracking, cyclotorsion, patient fixation), while decCV measures deviation from the anatomical reference. Because ImageJ-based literature reports decentration as vertex-referenced, decCV should be used for method comparilast studies.
Vertex fallback
At low resolution and with JPEG compression, the 1–2 pixel black dot gets washed out by surrounding white and cannot pass the val < 0.30 threshold; detection then jumps to another nearby dark pixel. In a real case this produced a 0.17 mm shift. For standard template images a fixed coordinate is used (two cases showed 0.50 and 0.20 px offset). Therefore detection is rejected if > 2.5 px from the ring center and the ring center is used instead; this is flagged as vertex source in the panel and CSV. Note: decTC is unaffected by this shift (it cancels in the difference), but decCV is directly affected — because the vertex is the reference point for that calculation. When vertex source is «ring», the decCV row is flagged.
Pupil center
Manually marked on the map. It is the zero point for Px/Py. The distance from pupil center to vertex is called pupil offset or chord mu in the literature.
TC (target center / intended centration point)
TC = pupil center + (Px, Py) Px/Py is the centration offset entered by the surgeon — not the full pupil-vertex offset measured by the device, but the applied offset. If Px=Py=0, the target is the pupil center. Coordinate axes: +x rightward, +y UPWARD — i.e. above the vertex is positive, below is negative y. If your device uses a different convention, toggle the sign button.
Do not confuse TC with ablation center. TC is the point the surgeon aimed at and is not measured — it is computed from the entered Px/Py. The ablation center (= EOZ center) is where the laser actually landed, measured from the map — i.e. the achieved treatment center. The difference between the two is decTC.
Residual centration gap
residual gap = |TC − vertex| The unapplied portion of the pupil-vertex offset. For eyes with no centration adjustment (Px=Py=0), this equals the pupil offset directly; when the offset is fully applied, it approaches zero. Can be used as a continuous independent variable in dose-response analysis across groups.

EOZ boundary

EOZ (effective optical zone)
The region enclosed by the green 0 D line on the tangential curvature difference map. In Pre−Post maps this region is warm (yellow–red): a hue < threshold mask is applied. In Post−Pre maps the treatment zone is cool (blue): a hue > threshold mask is applied and threshold defaults swap automatically (inner 149°, outer 125°). In both cases sat > 0.25, val > 0.30 are maintained.
Where the boundary is placed — 0 D band midline
The green band represents a range around zero; the true 0 D line is the center of the band. A single threshold (hue < 80°) sits on the inner edge only and systematically underestimates the radius. Therefore two thresholds are used by default: Pre−Post: inner hue < 125° / outer hue < 149° Post−Pre: inner hue > 149° / outer hue > 125° In Post−Pre the roles swap: the «outer» edge of pre-post (hue 149°) becomes the «inner» edge of post-pre. A separate ellipse is fitted to each, and the average of the two fits is taken (center and semi-axes arithmetic, angle via double-angle). The semi-axis difference is reported as green band width — the boundary uncertainty in that image.
Validation: on a real map, the dioptre values under the inner and outer contours measured +0.132 D and −0.132 D (expected ±step/2 = ±0.125). The midline yields −0.001 D with 84% of points inside the 0.00 D bin. Single-edge measurement deviates by ~band/2 in radius; area underestimates by approximately 5–8%. The edge option has therefore been removed — it only activates automatically as a fallback when the band cannot be resolved.
Ellipse fit
An ellipse is fitted to the contour points using the Halir–Flusser direct least-squares method. Robust to boundary noise; uses all contour points equally.
Zone irregularity (RMS residual)
Radial deviation of the inner contour from its own fitted ellipse: RMS = √( Σ d² / n ),  d = r · (1 − 1/ρ),  ρ = √((u/a)²+(v/b)²) Why a separate indicator is needed: the difference between ellipse center and area centroid is insensitive to irregularity — for symmetric irregularity, both centers coincide and the difference stays near zero. In a real case with visible contour irregularity (RMS 0.117 mm), center separation was only 0.015 mm — the old indicator would have missed it. The threshold (0.10 mm) is provisional, calibrated on only two eyes; validate on your own dataset.

Key results

EOZ area — contour (primary)
The area of the region bounded by the green contour, computed from the contour polygon (shoelace formula). This is the primary EOZ area and RR and equivalent diameter are derived from it — the quantity measured in published ImageJ workflows is also the region’s own area. Ellipse area (π · a · b) is reported secondarily. Because the Halir–Flusser boundary fit is not area-preserving, these two values can diverge; the further the ratio departs from 1, the worse the ellipse approximation. (In ImageJ the fit is area-preserving, so the two values are always identical.)
Major axis (2a) @ meridian
2a = the long diameter of the ellipse. Meridian is in clinical (TABO) convention: 0° horizontal, 90° vertical, increasing counterclockwise. meridian = θfit No additional mirroring is applied. The coordinate frame is set up with +y upward (image row index is inverted), so the fit angle is already counterclockwise — the same direction as TABO. The 180° − θ correction in earlier versions was removed because it would create double mirroring after the y-axis flip. The raw angle is also exported as theta_fit_deg in CSV for verification.
Minor axis (2b) @ meridian
2b = short diameter; its meridian is perpendicular to the major axis (+90°).
Decentration
decentration = | ablation center − CV | x and y components are reported separately. Ablation center = EOZ center (operational definition in the literature); it is not a separate measurement.
Eccentricity
Geometric (true): e = √(1 − b²/a²) range [0, 1); 0 for a circle. Axis ratio: a / b range [1, ∞); 1 for a circle. In the literature, «eccentricity» often means axis ratio; both are reported separately to avoid confusion.

Other values

a / b (semi-major / semi-minor)
Semi-axes, i.e. half the diameter. 2a and 2b are full axes.
Ablation center — two definitions
Ellipse fit center (default, consistent with literature) and area centroid (no shape assumption). If two-definition separation > 0.05 mm, the zone is irregular and the ellipse assumption weakens — that eye should be reviewed.
POZ
Programmed optical zone diameter. Entered manually; use the actual value from the device record. POZ area = π · (POZø/2)²
RR (retention ratio)
RR = 100 · EOZ area / POZ area Higher RR = LESS shrinkage (the name can be misleading). True reduction = 100 − RR
Perimeter (ellipse)
Computed using the Ramanujan approximation (no closed form exists for the ellipse perimeter).

Measurement uncertainty

Repeatability
Published data show that finding the optical zone center has good-to-excellent repeatability, but total decentration on tangential difference maps is only moderate (ICC ≈ 0.63; repeatability coefficient ≈ 0.25 mm). Inter-operator agreement limits for ImageJ-based methods have been reported as approximately −0.15 to +0.21 mm. Typical total decentration in KLEx is 0.18 ± 0.08 mm — meaning the effect size is on the same order as measurement noise. Interpret small inter-group differences in this context.
Hue threshold — what does it measure?
Hue angle has no fixed relationship to dioptres; thresholds must be calibrated to the palette. Measured swatch hues on the Oculus difference palette: +0.50 D → 96.5°   +0.25 D → 113.2°   0.00 D → 136.6°   −0.25 D → 162.3°   −0.50 D → 182.6° The previous defaults (80° / 175°) were effectively locating the +1.1 D and −0.35 D lines; the midline fell at approximately +0.37 D instead of 0.00 D, sitting inside the zone and systematically underestimating area. The current defaults (125° / 149°) are the MIDPOINTS of adjacent bins around 0.00 D, placing the band exactly on the 0.00 D bin; with these thresholds the hue method converges to within 0.1% of the scale method. Note: different palette/scale settings may require different thresholds — the scale method (default) is entirely independent of this issue.
